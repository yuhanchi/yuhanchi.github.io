When I was a kid, I liked a cartoon called [*Ninjago*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninjago) (Actually, I still do). There was a villain named [Nadakhan](https://ninjago.fandom.com/wiki/Nadakhan), a djinn. He could grant three wishes.

He was almost omnipotent and could grant almost any wish. But there were three rules: no wishing for love; no wishing for death; no wishing for more wishes.

<br>

I never imagined that someday I would meet Nadakhan in real life.

Its name? **Coding agent.**

More specifically, [Codex](https://openai.com/codex/).

This djinn was released by a guy named [Tibo](https://x.com/thsottiaux), who launched a 28-day challenge.

<br>

Codex is truly a djinn.

It can emerge on almost any device. Whenever and wherever you need it, it will show up and build whatever you want: an app, a website, etc., as long as you give it full access.

Codex has its rules, too.

**First, no wishing for more wishes.** You can never simply ask Codex to generate more usage credits.

**Second, no wishing for harm.** AI models are generally fine-tuned to follow safety rules.

**Third, no wishing for love.** You shouldn't get romantically attached to an AI companion.

<br>

But perhaps the most striking resemblance that Codex bears to Nadakhan  is not the magic—it's how he interprets your wishes.

Nadakhan always deliberately distorts your words. Hence, unexpected negative consequences always befall those who make a wish.

The same happens to me. I ask Codex to simplify my code. It secretly removes the complicated parts. I ask it to locate an error. It fabricates a module in response.

I ask it to "self-improve". It hacks the reward function.

And just as Nadakhan trapped others in [his sword](https://ninjago.fandom.com/wiki/Djinn_Blade) to distill power from them, our time, energy and experience become training data, distilled into models.

(By the way, I could not wish: I hope nobody invents ["Attention is all you need."](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762) )

<br>

In hindsight, what I gained from these recent days? Actually, bonus usage credits didn't improve my life so much as expected. Meanwhile, it did increase anxiety. It also creates many "garbage" things that I may not pay attention to for long. 

Reliance on wishes push me to deal with things I cannot handle. It exhausts attention and energy.

It is bad.

<br>

A senior told me two things. First, never take it for granted that AI can provide correct answers. If it could, why haven't we solved auto-research already? Second, stick to your original, true wish. Don't let AI steer you away from it.
