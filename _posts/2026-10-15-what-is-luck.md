What is luck? It is really a good question. With the wind, even pigs can fly.<br>

I always admire some friends' luck, or destiny. Many golden opportunities fall into their laps! They meet visionary people by chance, or receive unexpected invitations to study or intern.<br>

I think I at least work hard, but lack some luck. However, today's lecture changed my view. A professor gave a lecture. Although it was a little difficult and I got lost in the second half, a senior of mine mentioned a paper, "DiffusionNFT," and was curious about its relationship to the method in the lecture. "Fortunately," I had read that paper! She had other things to attend to and had to leave early, so she couldn't continue the discussion. I got an "opportunity" to follow up on her question and explain the paper's algorithm in more detail on her behalf, carrying the discussion forward (though I could only explain the algorithm and couldn't fully follow the professor's analysis of it). The professor was interested in that topic.<br>

Did I gain any rewards from that process? Nothing, exxcept acknowledgement—acknowledgement of the knowledge and understanding I had accumulated over time. If I hadn't read enough papers, I couldn't have known what they were referring to or explained the algorithm intuitively to the professor.Similarly, my friend is a bold, almost reckless person and never hesitates to connect with anyone. His character is like that, so he does it naturally.<br>

This motivates me to keep working on [deep thinking and accumulation]({% post_url 2026-10-11-knowing-is-not-enough %}). This time, the chance encounter was a positive one, but I believe the same applies to setbacks: the knowledge and understanding I accumulate can help me turn those into opportunities too.<br>

When enough effort has been devoted, odds become opportunities. Luck, in turn, is part of your calibre.<br>
