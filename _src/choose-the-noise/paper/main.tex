\begin{abstract}
\noindent A family of methods steers a pretrained flow-matching or diffusion policy without
changing its weights: a small proposer chooses the input noise for each state, and the frozen
generator turns that noise into an action. This note collects the family, which is not
grouped together in a recent survey of flow matching in reinforcement learning, and reads it
through a VAE-style lens: the proposer is an encoder, the frozen generator is a decoder, the
Gaussian is the prior, and value replaces reconstruction. For a KL-regularized objective the
connection is an exact variational identity. It then asks where reinforcement learning takes
effect, and organizes the methods by how they carry an action-space value back into noise space:
a noise critic, flow inversion, backpropagation through the sampler, a one-step decoder, or a
likelihood-ratio gradient. A two-dimensional experiment trains the same proposer with all five
routes. This PDF is a version of a blog post; it is not peer reviewed.
\end{abstract}
